## 1. What Silicon Valley-grade actually means

Not "sounds American." It means the name passes seven tests:

1. **The podcast test.** Heard once on a podcast, spellable on the first try. This alone kills accents, homophones, and clever spellings.
2. **Two to three syllables.** Vercel, Attio, Linear, Sierra, Notion, Ramp. Short enough to verb, long enough to own.
3. **The headline test.** "____ raises $40M Series A to bring judgment to AI creative work" must read naturally in TechCrunch.
4. **Domain honesty.** The get- prefix era is over; that pattern already dates getcaastor.co. Acceptable: exact .com, .ai for an AI-native product, or a with-/[name]hq fallback you are not embarrassed by at Series B.
5. **Category-far meaning.** The best names borrow meaning from another domain (Sierra, Clay, Cursor, Granola) or coin it (Vercel, Attio, Anthropic). Descriptive names die in commoditization; never put AI in the name.
6. **Phonetic neutrality across the top ten languages.** Spain is covered by this rule automatically, which is the correct direction of causality. v1 had it backwards.
7. **Room for a system.** The name must be able to hold a character (the Brandolph successor), a certification mark, and a plan family without strain.

## 2. What the screening just proved

I ran collision checks on eleven real-word candidates today. Results:

| Name | Verdict | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Signet | Dead | Multiple active companies incl. Signet Cyber, Signet Corporation ("The Seal of Trust"), plus Signet Jewelers at consumer scale |
| Sigil | Dead | Several funded startups under Sigil, Sigil Labs, Sigil Group |
| Lore | Dead | Heavily contested: lore.com (sovereign AI), Lore AI, Lore Machine |
| Braid | Dead | Multiple: dead Index/Accel fintech, braid-software.com, Braid Technologies |
| Auteur | Dead, and instructive | theauteur.ai markets itself as an "AI Creative Operating System." Direct category collision. |
| Tessera | Dead | Tessera Labs, ~$120M raised, a16z-led. Untouchable in AI. |
| Cachet | Dead | Insurtech Cachet plus CachetHQ open-source status pages |
| Marque | Dead, and instructive | marque.marketing sells "the operating system for modern marketing teams." Our category, that name, already claimed. |
| Insignia | Dead | Insignia Ventures Partners plus multiple Insignia software companies |
| Prova | Dead | Several active Prova companies incl. funded hiring startup |
| **Cerna** | **Survivor** | Prior holder Cerna Solutions rebranded to Thirdera. Residual risk: phonetic proximity to Cerner (being absorbed into Oracle Health, so the collision is shrinking, not growing) |

Two conclusions, one strategic:

**Naming conclusion:** evocative real English words in AI and creative software are saturated. This is exactly why the current generation coins (Vercel, Attio, Anthropic) or reaches into far domains. Our route is coined-first.

**Market intel, more important than the naming:** theauteur.ai and marque.marketing are both actively claiming the "creative/marketing operating system" position right now, with weaker products than ours as far as we can see, and neither with a certified human layer. The category window is open and being contested this quarter. Every month of naming deliberation is a month of category land-grab we watch from the bench. This hardens the earlier recommendation: contingency in the drawer, full speed on the live brand.

## 3. Three viable patterns

**Pattern A: coined latinate (primary recommendation).** Invented, meaning-rooted, ownable by construction. The trademark space is empty because you just made the word. Cost: meaning must be earned through marketing, which we would be doing anyway.

**Pattern B: far-domain real word.** A real word from a domain far from software, recontextualized (the Sierra/Clay move). Higher charm, brutal clearance odds, as the table above shows.

**Pattern C: character-led (the Harvey pattern).** AI-native companies increasingly carry a person-name (Harvey for legal). We already run this pattern internally with Brandolph. In a split where Brandolph's mark is lost, the successor character could BE the company. Keep as a wildcard; it couples the brand to one product surface.

## 4. The slate

All coined names below are constructed today and unscreened except where noted. Nothing here is a decision; it is the input to formal clearance (EUIPO + USPTO classes 35/42, domains, handles, native-speaker checks in the top ten languages).

### Pattern A: coined, judgment root

The thesis is human judgment entwined with machine speed. Latin gives us cernere (to sift, discern, decide) and veritas/vetting.

| Candidate | Construction | Why it works | Watch out |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cerna** | from cernere, to discern | Judgment in the root; two syllables; open vowel ending travels everywhere; the one name that already survived screening | Cerner phonetic shadow (fading into Oracle Health) |
| **Vetria** | vetted + veritas | Verification and truth in one coinage; very SaaS-native sound | Unscreened; -ia suffix is fashionable, verify it doesn't blur into pharma naming |
| **Sagia** | sage root, wisdom | Soft, warm, wise; counterweights the cold-AI category | Unscreened; may read feminine-personal-name in some markets |
| **Vervia** | verve + via | Creative energy with a route through it | Unscreened; Verve Group (adtech) owns the root word |

### Pattern A adjacent: coined, curation/taste root

Taste is the emerging category language: the argument that in the AI era, taste is the scarce asset. Owning a taste-rooted name positions against every volume-AI competitor.

| Candidate | Construction | Why it works | Watch out |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Curata** | curare, to care for and to curate | Curation is taste operationalized; caring is the human layer | A content-curation company used this name in the 2010s; verify it's dead and the mark lapsed |
| **Orenda** | Iroquois: the human force that changes the world | Arguably the most beautiful available meaning for a human-agency company | Unscreened; cultural-borrowing diligence required, not just legal |

### Pattern B: far-domain real words worth one screening pass each

| Candidate | Domain | The idea | Watch out |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Keel** | sailing | The unseen structure that keeps a fast vessel from capsizing: human judgment under AI speed | One syllable, brutal to clear; screen before loving |
| **Easel** | the studio | The human-built structure that holds the creative work | Likely contested in creative software |
| **Discern** | plain English | The thesis as a verb; verbability built in | Common word, weak mark without heavy use evidence |

### The method name (firewall move 1, decide independently of the company name)

**Phronesis**: Aristotle's term for practical wisdom, judgment in action. Too academic for a company; nearly perfect for the certification methodology ("BIOs are certified under the Phronesis Method"). A method can afford an intellectual name; it signals depth instead of asking for hype. Alternative: keep "BIO Method" if the BIO term is confirmed ours.

## 5. Ranking and next step

1. **Cerna.** The only candidate with a screening pass already behind it, the root meaning is the thesis, and it passes all seven tests. "Cerna raises $40M" reads like it was always there.
2. **Vetria.** Best unscreened coinage; verification root doubles as the certification story.
3. **Curata.** Highest strategic upside if the old mark is truly dead, because it buys the taste position outright.

Next step, one week of effort, then stop: formal clearance on these three (EUIPO, USPTO, domains, handles, native-speaker pass), pick the single survivor, acquire quietly, seal the envelope. Then all naming energy returns to the live brand, because section 2's market intel says the real race is not what we're called. It's who claims the category first.
